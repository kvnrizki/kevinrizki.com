---
title: Attention is a practice, not a resource
excerpt: The quality of what we make depends on what we notice. A few thoughts on protecting the space to notice it.
category: Reflection
date: "2026-10-08"
readingTime: 6
---

Attention is often described as a limited resource, like time or money. That makes it sound like something we spend until it is gone. I think of it more as a practice: something we can shape with the conditions we create around our work.

When every quiet moment is filled with a notification, a meeting, or another open tab, it gets harder to notice what is actually happening. The details that matter are usually not the loudest ones. They show up in a customer's hesitation, a recurring bug, or a sentence in a draft that does not quite sound true.

Protecting attention does not mean working without interruption forever. It can mean leaving a little space between meetings, writing down the question before opening a new tool, or giving one useful task your full focus for a while.

The result is not just more work completed. It is a better chance of seeing the work clearly enough to make it worthwhile.
